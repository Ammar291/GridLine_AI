These profiles summarise field surveys, borehole logs and infiltration tests carried out by the State
Geological Survey, Nandipur District Office. Five geological zones are recognised: GZ-1 Tekri Quartzite Ridge,
GZ-2 Hillview Phyllite Slopes, GZ-3 Kalinadi Recent Alluvium, GZ-4 Older Alluvial Terrace and GZ-5 Nandi
Lacustrine Basin. Values are representative for each zone; site investigation is required for design. Slope
angles are given as mean / maximum.

## 1 Hillview (Z-HV)

Geological zone GZ-2, Hillview Phyllite Slopes. Soil: colluvial silty clay over weathered phyllite, with
colluvium 4.5 m deep over bedrock on the mid-slopes. Infiltration rate 8 mm/h; plasticity index 22; bearing
capacity 120 kPa. The water table lies about 3 m below ground and becomes perched within the colluvium after
heavy rain. Mapped slopes: SL-HV-1 Hillview Terrace slope 32° / 38°; SL-HV-2 lower terrace slope 28° / 34°;
SL-HV-3 Hill Road cut at km 2.1, 41° / 55°. The phyllite dips to the south-west, parallel to the slopes. Hazard
notes: the colluvium is slide-prone when saturated; cuts reduce toe support; runoff and debris reach the
Kalinadi drain D-7 at the foot of the ridge.

## 2 Tekri Heights (Z-TH)

Geological zone GZ-1, Tekri Quartzite Ridge. Soil: thin residual soil over hard quartzite, typically 1.2 m to
bedrock. Infiltration rate 5 mm/h; bearing capacity 300 kPa on rock; water table deeper than 12 m except in
gullies. Mapped slopes: SL-TH-1 Tekri Quarry face 55° / 70°; SL-TH-2 Tekri Nala gully slopes 30° / 40°; SL-TH-3
Quarry Road hairpins 36° / 48°. Hazard notes: rockfall and bench failure on quarry and road cuts; very high
runoff from bare rock into the Tekri Nala (R-2) and the head of D-7; the check dams DM-3 trap sediment.
Vegetation loss on the quarry face increases erosion and debris supply.

## 3 Riverside (Z-RS)

Geological zone GZ-3, Kalinadi Recent Alluvium. Soil: alluvial silt and sand deposited by the Kalinadi,
about 25 m to bedrock. Infiltration rate 15 mm/h near the surface, falling quickly as the ground saturates;
bearing capacity 90 kPa; water table 2.0 m below ground in the dry season and near the surface during floods.
No slopes steeper than 5° are mapped. Hazard notes: the zone lies largely on the flood plain FP-1; moderate
liquefaction susceptibility in loose saturated sand; bank erosion along the Kalinadi; drainage by D-8 and the
lower reach of D-7 is limited when the river is high.

## 4 Old Town (Z-OT)

Geological zone GZ-3, Kalinadi Recent Alluvium. Soil: alluvial clay loam with old made ground up to 3 m thick
in the core, about 22 m to bedrock. Infiltration rate 6 mm/h; bearing capacity 100 kPa; water table 2.5 m below
ground. No mapped slopes; ground falls gently to the Kalinadi riverfront. Hazard notes: the riverfront lies on
the flood plain FP-3 behind an earth embankment; old brick drains such as D-3 are prone to silting; dense
building on made ground is sensitive to settlement; narrow lanes limit access. Moderate liquefaction
susceptibility near the river.

## 5 Market Ward (Z-MW)

Geological zone GZ-4, Older Alluvial Terrace. Soil: compact clay loam, about 18 m to bedrock. Infiltration rate
7 mm/h; bearing capacity 150 kPa; water table 4 m below ground. No mapped slopes; the ground falls gently south
towards Old Town. Hazard notes: good bearing and low liquefaction susceptibility; a very high share of paved
surface means rainfall runs off quickly to the covered drain D-4; deep basements need dewatering, which
discharges to drains and can lower the local water table. Settlement of adjacent buildings shall be monitored
during deep excavation.

## 6 Station Road (Z-SR)

Geological zone GZ-4, Older Alluvial Terrace. Soil: silty loam over railway fill up to 4 m thick, about 20 m to
bedrock. Infiltration rate 9 mm/h; bearing capacity 130 kPa; water table 3.5 m below ground, rising to about
2 m in the monsoon. No mapped slopes. Hazard notes: the rail underpass TU-1, 4.5 m below grade, lies below the
monsoon water table and depends on sump pumps; the culverted drain D-5 surcharges in intense rain; the railway
embankment blocks natural drainage and ponds water on its upslope side.

## 7 Civil Lines (Z-CL)

Geological zone GZ-4, Older Alluvial Terrace, rising to shale on the Civil Lines Rise. Soil: lateritic gravel
over shale, about 8 m to bedrock. Infiltration rate 20 mm/h; bearing capacity 250 kPa; water table 8 m below
ground. Mapped slope: SL-CL-1 Civil Lines scarp 15° / 20°, stable, with good vegetation cover. Hazard notes: the
highest and best-drained ground in the city, with low flood and landslide susceptibility; minor erosion of the
scarp face where surface water is concentrated; suitable for critical facilities and shelters.

## 8 Lakeside (Z-LK)

Geological zone GZ-5, Nandi Lacustrine Basin. Soil: soft lacustrine clay and peat, about 30 m to bedrock.
Infiltration rate 2 mm/h; bearing capacity 45 kPa; water table 0.8 m below ground and at the surface in the
monsoon. No mapped slopes; the basin floor lies below the Kalinadi bank level. Hazard notes: the basin drains
only through the regulated outlet DM-2 and the pumped drain D-11, so standing water persists after heavy rain;
poor bearing and ongoing subsidence under fill and buildings; waterlogging damages foundations and roads.

## 9 New Colony (Z-NC)

Geological zone GZ-3, Kalinadi Recent Alluvium. Soil: alluvial silt on former paddy land, about 26 m to bedrock.
Infiltration rate 10 mm/h; bearing capacity 80 kPa; water table 1.5 m below ground, rising close to the surface
in the monsoon. No mapped slopes; the land is flat and low on the south bank. Hazard notes: the zone lies largely
on the flood plain FP-2 with no embankment; infiltration falls sharply once the silt is saturated; conversion
of paddy to paved surface increases runoff to D-9; moderate liquefaction susceptibility.

## 10 Mill Road Industrial (Z-MI)

Geological zone GZ-3, Kalinadi Recent Alluvium. Soil: alluvial sand and gravel, about 24 m to bedrock.
Infiltration rate 25 mm/h; bearing capacity 140 kPa; water table 2.2 m below ground. No mapped slopes. Hazard
notes: part of the flood plain FP-2; permeable ground allows spilled chemicals and fuel to reach groundwater
quickly; the open drain D-12 carries industrial runoff and is prone to blockage; moderate liquefaction
susceptibility in loose saturated sand; industrial stores of solvents and fuel raise urban fire hazard.
