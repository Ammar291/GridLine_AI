This procedure is issued by NMC-DMC with the State Geological Survey, Nandipur District Office. It applies the
landslide thresholds of the Nandipur Disaster Management Policy to day-to-day slope monitoring.

## 1 Purpose and scope

This procedure covers the monitored slopes of Hillview (Z-HV), Tekri Heights (Z-TH) and Civil Lines (Z-CL): SL-HV-1,
SL-HV-2 and SL-HV-3 on the Hillview Ridge, SL-TH-1, SL-TH-2 and SL-TH-3 on Tekri Hill, and SL-CL-1 on the Civil
Lines scarp. It applies to the Duty Officer, the Hill Rescue Team (C-4), the Road Maintenance Crew (C-3) and every
permit holder working on these slopes.

## 2 Rainfall inspection triggers

The Duty Officer shall check the 24-hour rainfall at RG-02 (Hillview) and RG-01 (Tekri) every hour from 1 June to
30 September. When 24-hour rainfall reaches 65 mm, C-4 shall inspect the slopes above 25 degrees within 12 hours.
At 115 mm, inspections shall be made every 6 hours and RD-01 cut slopes checked before each shift. At 175 mm,
C-4 shall inspect continuously where safe and the Duty Officer shall request a landslide critical review.

## 3 Soil moisture probe readings

Probes SM-01 (SL-HV-1 upper), SM-02 (SL-HV-1 toe), SM-03 (SL-HV-2) and, once installed, SM-04 (SL-TH-1 crest)
report saturation as a fraction every 15 minutes. A saturation reading of 0.70 or above sets landslide warning
for the slope and a reading of 0.85 or above sets critical, provided the value is confirmed by two consecutive
readings. A probe that reports a jump of more than 0.20 in 15 minutes, or no reading for 30 minutes, shall be
flagged as suspect and checked in the field by C-4.

## 4 Landslide index

The Duty Officer shall band each hillside zone by the landslide index computed by the EOC: watch at 0.35,
warning at 0.55 and critical at 0.75. The index combines rainfall, saturation, slope angle and site condition.
Where the index and a direct reading in sections 2 or 3 point to different bands, the higher band applies. The
index shall be recomputed after every new rainfall or probe reading.

## 5 Slope inspection procedure

An inspection by C-4 shall record, for each slope: new cracks at the crest, bulging or seepage at the toe,
fresh rockfall, blocked surface drains, the state of retaining walls and mesh, and any excavation left
unsupported. Findings shall be reported to the EOC by radio at once and in writing within 2 hours. C-4 shall not
enter a slope at landslide critical without the approval of the Incident Commander.

## 6 Debris protection of D-7

The Kalinadi drain (D-7) runs below SL-HV-1, SL-HV-2 and SL-HV-3 and receives runoff from the Tekri slopes. At
landslide warning, the Drainage Crew (C-2) shall inspect D-7 from its head to the BR-4 crossing and keep a
mobile excavator ready with C-3. Any debris entering D-7 shall be reported at once and removed as a first
priority, because a blockage raises flood risk downstream in Riverside (Z-RS). Trash racks shall be cleared
after every storm.

## 7 Construction on steep slopes

When 24-hour rainfall at the gauge serving a site reaches 65 mm, or saturation at any probe on or below the site
reaches 0.70, the Duty Officer shall notify the permit holder and request a halt of all earthworks on slopes above
25 degrees under the construction halt rule of the Disaster Management Policy. C-4 shall verify on site that machinery has stopped. Work may resume only
after clearance by NMC-DMC following an inspection.

## 8 Reporting and records

The Duty Officer shall keep a daily slope log for the monsoon season with rainfall, saturation readings,
landslide index, inspections and actions. The log shall be sent weekly to the State Geological Survey, which
shall review the probe calibration each year before 1 June and advise NMC-DMC on any slope whose stability
class should change.
