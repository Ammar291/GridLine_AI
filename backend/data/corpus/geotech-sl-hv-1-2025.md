This assessment was prepared by the State Geological Survey, Nandipur District Office, at the request of the
NMC Town Planning Department, for the hazard review of the permit application for Hillview Terrace Phase 2
(PR-HT2) under pol-construction-hazard-2025 section 2. It draws on four boreholes drilled in October 2025,
laboratory tests on colluvium samples, the readings of soil moisture probes SM-01 and SM-02 since 2020 and the
Hillview profile in geo-profiles-2020.

## 1 Site and slope geometry

SL-HV-1, the Hillview Terrace slope, lies on the south-west face of Hillview Ridge (HL-2) in Hillview (Z-HV).
It is 260 m long measured along the ground and rises about 140 m, with a mean angle of 32 degrees and a maximum
of 38 degrees on its upper third. The proposed site covers 18,500 m2 of the mid-slope, with a planned maximum
excavation depth of 6.0 m for building platforms. The toe of the slope lies about 60 m from the bank of the
Kalinadi drain (D-7). Vegetation covered about 45 % of the slope at the time of the fieldwork. The Tekri Hill
Reservoir (WF-3) stands about 400 m upslope.

## 2 Ground model

The boreholes found colluvial silty clay with phyllite fragments, 4.5 m thick at mid-slope and thickening
towards the D-7 valley, over weathered phyllite that becomes slightly weathered below about 9 m. The foliation
of the phyllite dips to the south-west at about 30 degrees, close to parallel with the slope face. The most
likely slip surface is at or near the base of the colluvium, where the weathered rock is less permeable. The
water table stood 3.0 m below ground in October 2025. After heavy rain, water perches within the colluvium above
the weathered rock and the colluvium approaches full saturation.

## 3 Soil strength

Laboratory tests on twelve undisturbed samples of the colluvium gave a unit weight of 19 kN/m3, a plasticity
index of 22, an effective cohesion of 4 kPa and a peak effective friction angle of 31 degrees. Ring shear tests
gave a residual friction angle of 24 degrees, which applies where the colluvium has moved before or is sheared
by excavation. The bearing capacity for shallow foundations on the colluvium is 120 kPa and the infiltration
rate 8 mm/h, as in geo-profiles-2020. The weathered phyllite is markedly stronger than the colluvium and was
not the controlling material in any analysis.

## 4 Groundwater response

SM-01 on the upper slope and SM-02 near the toe have reported saturation since 2020. In the dry season both
read between 0.30 and 0.45. During wet spells saturation rises by about 0.25 for every 100 mm of rain, and it
falls back over one to two days once rain stops, faster on the upper slope than at the toe. The highest readings
on record are 0.78 at SM-02 and 0.74 at SM-01, both during the September 2021 flood. Where vegetation is cleared
and the colluvium is exposed in a cut face, rain enters the ground faster and saturation rises more quickly
than these records show.

## 5 Stability of the existing slope

Limit-equilibrium analyses along the base of the colluvium give the following factors of safety (FoS) for the
slope as it stood in 2025, by saturation at SM-01 and SM-02:

| Saturation | FoS |
|---|---|
| 0.40 or less | 1.46 |
| 0.55 | 1.34 |
| 0.70 | 1.22 |
| 0.85 | 1.08 |

This assessment uses a minimum FoS of 1.5 for permanent works and 1.3 during construction; a FoS of 1.0 is the
point of failure. The existing slope falls below 1.3 above a saturation of about 0.60, which is why it is
classed as marginal.

## 6 Stability during bench excavation

Factors of safety for excavation on the site, by the height of cut face left unsupported and by saturation:

| Unsupported cut face | Saturation | FoS |
|---|---|---|
| one 1.5 m bench | 0.55 | 1.30 |
| one 1.5 m bench | 0.70 | 1.15 |
| 3.0 m | 0.55 | 1.17 |
| 3.0 m | 0.70 | 1.02 |
| 3.0 m | 0.85 | 0.91 |
| none: 6.0 m behind a completed retaining wall with drainage | 0.85 | 1.41 |

Each additional 1.5 m of unsupported face lowers the FoS by about 0.13 at a saturation of 0.70.

## 7 Design measures assumed

The factors of safety for excavation in section 6 hold only with the following measures: benches no higher than
1.5 m, each supported and drained before the next is cut; no cut face left unsupported for more than 14 days;
the permanent retaining wall at the downslope edge of the excavation completed before the monsoon; interceptor
drains above the excavation leading to a lined outlet; sheeting on exposed faces when rain is forecast; no spoil
placed on the slope below the works; and vegetation kept on the slope outside the working area. These measures
were passed to the Town Planning Department for the hazard review.

## 8 Limits of the assessment

The analyses assume the ground model of section 2 across the whole site and no water entering the slope from
above other than rainfall. They do not model earthquake loading or leakage from buried services. The minor leak
at the Tekri Hill Reservoir (WF-3) found in August 2023 had been repaired before the boreholes were drilled. The
results should be reviewed if the excavation plan changes, if cracks, bulging or seepage appear on the slope, or
if saturation readings rise above the range covered by the tables in sections 5 and 6.
