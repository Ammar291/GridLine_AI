This policy sets how the Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) allocates limited resources between competing needs
during an incident in Nandipur.

## 1 Purpose and scope

This policy applies to mobile pumps, crews, ambulances, hospital beds, shelters, generators and fuel owned or
controlled by NMC and its partners. It sets the priorities the Incident Commander shall apply when requests
exceed the resources available. Life safety always comes first; protection of critical infrastructure comes
next; protection of property comes last.

## 2 Priority tiers

Assets on the critical infrastructure register are ranked in three tiers. Tier 1 assets (hospitals H-1 and H-2,
substations PS-1 and PS-2, water treatment plant WF-1, bridge BR-1, Hill Road RD-01, the Kalinadi drain D-7 and
fire station FS-1) shall be protected first. Tier 2 assets (such as H-5, PS-5, WF-2, WF-3 and the D-11 pumping
station) come next, then tier 3 assets such as TU-1. Within a tier, the asset serving the larger population has
priority.

## 3 Mobile pumps

NMC holds 4 mobile diesel pumps, PU-M1 to PU-M4, each rated at 0.6 m³/s, giving 2.4 m³/s in total. Pumps are
kept at the Old Town works yard and operated by the Drainage Crew (C-2). The Incident Commander shall allocate
them to the priority sites in pol-flood-response-2024 section 7 and shall keep at least one pump in reserve
until a second site reaches flood warning. Pumps shall not be split across more than 3 sites at once.

## 4 Crew roles

Crews shall be used for their primary role unless the Incident Commander records a reason: C-1 Rescue Alpha for
search, rescue and boats; C-2 Drainage Crew for pumps and channel clearing; C-3 Road Maintenance Crew for debris
and barricades; C-4 Hill Rescue Team for slope inspection and rope rescue in Z-HV and Z-TH; C-5 Medical Response
Team for triage and shelter visits; C-6 Electrical Repair Crew for substations; C-7 Boat Rescue Unit for Lakeside
and flood plains; C-8 New Colony Volunteer Team for door-to-door warnings and sandbags.

## 5 Ambulances and hospital beds

The city fleet of 14 ambulances shall be dispatched through ambulance control, with at least 2 ambulances held
for each zone at warning or critical. When an incident is at L3 or above, each hospital shall release at least
20 % of its general beds for casualties within 6 hours by discharging or transferring stable patients. The
District Hospital H-1 shall receive transfers from H-2 and H-4 when either must reduce services.

## 6 Generators and fuel

Every hospital, shelter with a generator, pumping station and water treatment plant shall hold generator fuel
for at least 48 hours of full-load running from 1 June to 31 October. When a zone reaches warning, the EOC shall
check fuel stocks at every such site and order refuelling where less than 48 hours remain. Fuel for mobile pumps
shall be carried by C-2 for 12 hours of running per pump.

## 7 Competing requests

When two requests cannot both be met, the Incident Commander shall allocate the resource to the request that
protects more lives; where lives are not at stake, to the higher-tier asset; and where tiers are equal, to the
zone at the higher band. Each allocation decision and its reason shall be recorded. Resources shall be
reallocated as soon as the need that justified them has passed.

## 8 Mutual aid

When local resources are exhausted, the Emergency Operations Director may request pumps, boats, crews or
ambulances from the district administration and neighbouring municipalities. Resources received through mutual
aid shall be placed under the Incident Commander and used under this policy. Their arrival, use and return
shall be recorded.
