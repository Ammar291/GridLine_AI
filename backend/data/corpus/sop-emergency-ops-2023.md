These procedures govern the daily and emergency operation of the Emergency Operations Centre (EOC) of NMC-DMC,
located in Civil Lines (Z-CL) and linked to the city control room at the Kotwali police station.

## 1 Purpose and scope

These procedures describe how the EOC monitors Nandipur, how it moves between activation modes, and how it
directs and records the response to an incident. They apply to the Duty Officer, the Incident Commander, the
Emergency Operations Director and the liaison officers of NMC departments, the police, the fire service,
hospitals and utilities.

## 2 Activation modes

The EOC operates in three modes. Monitoring mode: one Duty Officer on shift, used when all zones are normal or at
watch. Partial activation: the Duty Officer, an Incident Commander and liaison officers for the affected
services, used when any zone is at warning. Full activation: all positions staffed and the Emergency Operations
Director in charge, used when any zone is at critical or an evacuation order is in force.

## 3 Duty Officer watch routine

The Duty Officer shall review all sensor readings at least every 15 minutes, and every 5 minutes when any zone is
at warning. The Duty Officer shall check the landslide thresholds (index 0.35, 0.55, 0.75; 24-hour rainfall 65,
115 and 175 mm; saturation 0.70 and 0.85), the flood thresholds (index 0.30, 0.50, 0.70; RV-01 stage 4.2, 5.0
and 5.5 m), one-hour rainfall of 30 and 50 mm/h and wind of 62, 89 and 118 km/h, and record every change of band.

## 4 Incident command

When any zone reaches warning, the Duty Officer shall open an incident and call in the Incident Commander on
duty. The Incident Commander shall set objectives, assign crews and resources, and approve road closures and
pump deployments. For each incident a single log shall be kept, recording every reading relied upon, every
decision, the role that approved it, and the outcome observed afterwards.

## 5 Situation reports

The EOC shall issue a situation report every 3 hours during partial activation and every hour during full
activation. Each report shall state the bands per zone, the latest key readings, roads closed, shelters open with
occupancy, crews and pumps deployed, casualties and damage known, and the actions planned for the next period.
Reports shall be sent to the Commissioner of NMC, all departments and the district administration.

## 6 Warnings to the public

Public alerts shall follow the four alert levels of the Disaster Management Policy: advisory, watch, warning and
evacuate. Each alert shall name the zones, the hazard, what residents should do and which shelters are open. The
Duty Officer shall send alerts through the city control room, the 112 service, sirens, text messages and ward
volunteers, and shall confirm that each channel has delivered the message.

## 7 Coordination with services

At partial activation, liaison officers shall report every 2 hours on: hospital status and beds at H-1 to H-5;
power at substations PS-1 to PS-5; water facilities WF-1 to WF-6; fire stations FS-1 to FS-3; and police
stations. Any loss of power or water at a hospital or pumping station shall be reported to the Incident
Commander at once.

## 8 Checks before field dispatch

Before any crew, pump or ambulance is dispatched, the EOC shall check the current status of the roads on its
route, including closures, water on roads, debris and bridge status, and shall choose a route that is open. The
dispatch record shall name the route. Where the only route is closed or doubtful, the Incident Commander shall
decide whether to dispatch, reroute or pre-position resources.

## 9 Stand-down

The EOC shall return to a lower mode when the zones that required it have been lowered in line with the band
hysteresis rule. Before full stand-down, all crews, pumps and vehicles shall be accounted for, shelters closed or
handed over, roads reopened or their closure handed to the Roads Division, and the incident log closed.
