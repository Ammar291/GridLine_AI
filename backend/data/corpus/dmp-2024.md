This policy is issued by the NMC Disaster Management Cell (NMC-DMC) under the authority of the Nandipur Municipal
Corporation (NMC). It replaces the Disaster Management Policy of 2019 (dmp-2019) in full. Sector policies and
standard operating procedures issued by NMC-DMC shall apply the thresholds set out in section 4 and shall not
set weaker values.

## 1 Purpose and scope

This policy governs the preparation for, detection of, response to and recovery from floods, flash floods,
landslides, cyclones and urban fires in all ten zones of Nandipur, from Hillview (Z-HV) and Tekri Heights (Z-TH)
to Lakeside (Z-LK) and Mill Road Industrial (Z-MI). It applies to every NMC department, to the Emergency
Operations Centre (EOC), to contractors working for NMC and to every holder of a construction permit issued by
the NMC Town Planning Department. Where a sector policy conflicts with this policy, this policy prevails.

## 2 Roles and authority

The Duty Officer of the EOC shall monitor live sensor readings at all times, assign hazard bands under section 4
and issue advisories. The Incident Commander shall direct field response for an incident, including crew
dispatch, road closures and pump deployment. The Emergency Operations Director shall approve evacuation orders,
construction halts and activation of the full EOC. The Head of NMC-DMC shall maintain this policy and the
thresholds in section 4. Every decision taken under this policy shall be recorded with the time, the role that
approved it and the readings it relied on.

## 3 Monitoring network

NMC-DMC shall maintain rain gauges RG-01 to RG-05, soil moisture probes SM-01 to SM-04, channel level sensors
CL-D7, CL-D3 and CL-D11, river gauges RV-01 and RV-02, wind sensor WS-01 and lake level sensor LL-01. River
stage for all thresholds in this policy is read at RV-01 (Kalinadi at BR-1), measured above gauge zero. A sensor
that has not reported for 30 minutes shall be treated as unavailable and the Duty Officer shall use the nearest
working sensor of the same kind and record the substitution.

## 4 Hazard thresholds and bands

Section 4 sets the binding numeric thresholds. Each hazard is assigned one of four bands: normal, watch, warning
or critical.

## 4.1 Bands and hysteresis

A hazard band is raised as soon as any single threshold for that band is met or exceeded. A band shall be
lowered only when every reading that raised it has stayed below the threshold of the lower band for at least
6 hours continuously; a single reading below the threshold is not sufficient. Bands are assigned per zone. When
two thresholds for the same zone point to different bands, the higher band applies. The Duty Officer shall
record every change of band, the reading that caused it and the time. Lowering a critical band requires the
approval of the Incident Commander.

## 4.2 Landslide thresholds

For every zone containing slopes above 25 degrees, including Z-HV and Z-TH, the landslide index shall be
banded as follows: watch at landslide index ≥ 0.35, warning at ≥ 0.55 and critical at ≥ 0.75. Independently of
the index, 24-hour rainfall at the rain gauge serving the slope (RG-02 for Hillview, RG-01 for Tekri Heights)
raises the band to watch at ≥ 65 mm, warning at ≥ 115 mm and critical at ≥ 175 mm. Soil saturation at any probe
on a slope above 25 degrees (SM-01 to SM-04) raises the band to warning at ≥ 0.70 and critical at ≥ 0.85.

## 4.3 Flood thresholds

The flood index for each zone shall be banded as follows: watch at flood index ≥ 0.30, warning at ≥ 0.50 and
critical at ≥ 0.70. Independently of the index, the Kalinadi river stage at RV-01 raises the riverine zones
(Z-RS, Z-OT, Z-LK, Z-NC, Z-MI) to watch at ≥ 4.2 m (flood stage), to warning at ≥ 5.0 m, at which BR-1 is
closed, and to critical at ≥ 5.5 m (danger stage). Channel flow and shelter rules that depend on these bands are
set in pol-flood-response-2024 and pol-shelter-activation-2024.

## 4.4 Flash flood thresholds

For flash floods, rainfall intensity measured over one hour at any rain gauge raises the zone served by that
gauge to watch at ≥ 30 mm/h and to warning at ≥ 50 mm/h. A flash flood warning becomes critical when, in
addition, a channel in the zone is at or above its capacity or water is reported on a road or in a tunnel,
including TU-1. Zones with a high share of impermeable surface, and TU-1 in Station Road (Z-SR), shall be
checked first.

## 4.5 Cyclone thresholds

Sustained wind speed measured at WS-01 or forecast by the regional weather service raises all zones to cyclone
watch at ≥ 62 km/h, to cyclone warning at ≥ 89 km/h and to cyclone critical at ≥ 118 km/h. A cyclone warning
shall also trigger a review of the flood bands under section 4.3, because cyclone rainfall and river stage rise
together. At cyclone critical, all crews shall be withdrawn from exposed field work except life-saving
operations approved by the Incident Commander.

## 4.6 Urban fire levels

Urban fires are graded by the Fire Officer in charge. Level 1: a fire confined to one building, handled by one
station. Level 2: a fire spreading to adjacent buildings or needing appliances from two stations. Level 3: a
fire involving a market, warehouse cluster or hazardous materials, or needing all three stations FS-1, FS-2 and
FS-3. Level 4: a fire threatening a hospital, substation or shelter, or requiring evacuation of residents. Level
2 corresponds to watch, Level 3 to warning and Level 4 to critical. Fires in lanes narrower than 6.0 m shall be
graded one level higher.

## 5 Construction halt rule on steep slopes

All excavation, cutting, filling and heavy machinery work on any slope steeper than 25 degrees shall be halted
when the 24-hour rainfall at the rain gauge serving the site is ≥ 65 mm, or when soil saturation at any probe on
or below the site is ≥ 0.70. The halt applies immediately, whatever the permit says, and is ordered by the
Emergency Operations Director. Work may resume only after both readings have stayed below these values for 48
hours and the slope has been inspected by the Hill Rescue Team (C-4) or a geotechnical engineer approved by
NMC-DMC.

## 6 Alert levels

Public alerts shall use four levels. Advisory: issued by the Duty Officer when any zone reaches watch;
residents are informed and asked to follow updates. Watch: issued when any zone reaches warning; residents
prepare to move and vulnerable persons are assisted first. Warning: issued when any zone reaches critical;
movement to shelters is recommended and roads may close. Evacuate: an order approved by the Emergency Operations
Director for named zones or areas. Alerts shall be issued through the city control room, the 112 service,
sirens and ward volunteers, including the New Colony Volunteer Team (C-8).

## 7 Protection of critical infrastructure

Owners of tier 1 critical infrastructure, including hospitals H-1 and H-2, substations PS-1 and PS-2, water
treatment plant WF-1, bridge BR-1, Hill Road (RD-01) and the Kalinadi drain (D-7), shall keep an emergency plan
consistent with this policy and shall report their status to the EOC every 2 hours while any zone they serve is
at warning or critical. Hospitals shall keep backup power for at least 12 hours. Any work that alters the
capacity of a drainage channel shall be notified to NMC-DMC before it starts.

## 8 Review

NMC-DMC shall review this policy and the thresholds in section 4 every three years and after every incident of
severity 4 or above. Thresholds may be changed only by a new version of this policy approved by the Emergency
Operations Director and the Commissioner of NMC. Post-incident reviews shall state whether the thresholds
worked, and sector policies shall be updated within 90 days of any change to section 4.
