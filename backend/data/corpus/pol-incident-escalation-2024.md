This policy sets how incidents in Nandipur are escalated and who approves each kind of action. It applies the
bands of the Nandipur Disaster Management Policy (dmp-2024, section 4).

## 1 Purpose and scope

This policy applies to every incident handled by the Emergency Operations Centre (EOC), whatever the hazard. It
defines four incident levels, links them to the hazard bands, and states which role shall approve each kind of
action. No action listed in this policy shall be carried out without a recorded approval by the role named for
it, except where a person on the scene acts to save life.

## 2 Incident levels

Incidents shall be graded on four levels. L1 corresponds to the normal band: routine monitoring and
maintenance. L2 corresponds to the watch band: heightened monitoring, advisories and standby of crews. L3
corresponds to the warning band: active response, road closures, pump deployment and shelter activation. L4
corresponds to the critical band: full EOC activation, evacuation and protection of life and critical
infrastructure. The level of an incident equals the highest band of any zone it affects.

## 3 Triggers

An incident is escalated as soon as any threshold of dmp-2024 section 4 for the next band is met in any
affected zone, for example a landslide index of 0.55 or a Kalinadi stage of 5.0 m for L3. The Duty Officer may
escalate further on judgement when a channel overflows, a critical facility loses power or a road that is the
only access to a zone is blocked, and shall record the reason.

## 4 Advisories

The Duty Officer shall approve and issue advisories to the public and to departments for L2 incidents and above.
Advisories shall name the zones, the hazard, the band and the readings that caused it. The Duty Officer shall
also approve standby orders for crews and the opening of the EOC in monitoring mode. Advisories shall be
reviewed every 3 hours while the incident remains open.

## 5 Road closures and pump deployment

The Incident Commander shall approve road closures under pol-road-closure-2021, the deployment of mobile pumps
PU-M1 to PU-M4 under sop-pump-deployment-2022, the dispatch of crews C-1 to C-8, and the activation of shelters
under pol-shelter-activation-2024. The Incident Commander may delegate crew dispatch to a sector lead but shall
remain accountable for it. Each approval shall be recorded with the time and the expected outcome.

## 6 Evacuation orders and construction halts

The Emergency Operations Director shall approve every evacuation order, every order halting construction under
dmp-2024 section 5 or pol-construction-hazard-2025, the evacuation of a hospital, and the full activation of the
EOC for L4 incidents. Where the Emergency Operations Director cannot be reached within 15 minutes, the
deputy designated in the EOC roster shall exercise this authority and the decision shall be recorded as
delegated.

## 7 Failed actions and re-planning

Every approved action shall have a stated expected result that can be checked from sensor readings or field
reports. When a check shows that an action has failed or has not had its expected effect, the Incident
Commander shall prepare a revised plan within 30 minutes and submit any new action for approval under this
policy. A failed action shall not be repeated unchanged without recording why it is expected to succeed.

## 8 De-escalation and closure

An incident shall be de-escalated in line with the hysteresis rule of dmp-2024 section 4.1. It shall be closed
when all affected zones have returned to normal for 24 hours, all roads and shelters have been restored, and all
crews and pumps have returned to base. The Incident Commander shall file a closing report within 7 days.
