This procedure is issued by the NMC Drainage Division. It governs the use of the city's four mobile diesel pumps
and their support to fixed pumping stations during floods and flash floods.

## 1 Purpose and scope

This procedure applies to the mobile pumps PU-M1, PU-M2, PU-M3 and PU-M4 and to the Drainage Crew Old Town (C-2)
that operates them. It sets how pumps are requested, approved, transported, set up, run and recovered. It also
sets how mobile pumps support the fixed pumps PU-T1 and PU-T2 at the TU-1 sump and PU-L1 to PU-L3 at the D-11
station.

## 2 Pump fleet

Each mobile pump is a trailer-mounted diesel unit rated at 0.6 m³/s, with suction and delivery hoses, a lighting
mast and a fuel tank for 12 hours of running. The pumps are kept at the Old Town works yard in Old Town (Z-OT).
C-2 shall test-run every pump for 30 minutes each week from 1 May to 31 October and record the result. A pump
that fails its test shall be marked unavailable.

## 3 Request and approval

A request for a mobile pump shall be made to the EOC, stating the site, the channel or low point, the expected
duration and the discharge point. The Incident Commander shall approve every deployment. Where two sites compete,
the priority order is: the D-7 outfall and the Riverside low points it serves; the TU-1 sump in Station Road
(Z-SR); and Lakeside (Z-LK) along D-11.

## 4 Transport

Before a pump leaves the yard, C-2 shall check the road status on the route with the EOC and confirm that the
route has no closure and no water of 0.3 m or more. Pumps to Riverside shall travel by RD-02 while it is open;
pumps to Hillview shall travel by RD-01. Transport time shall be recorded from departure to arrival on site.

## 5 Setup

On arrival, the crew shall place the pump on firm ground above the expected water level, lay the suction hose
to the sump or low point, lay the delivery hose to a discharge point downstream that is not surcharged, and
secure the site with barriers and lights. Setup shall be completed within 45 minutes of arrival. The crew shall
report to the EOC when the pump starts discharging.

## 6 Operation

While running, the crew shall check the pump, fuel level, suction and discharge every hour and report the water
level at the site to the EOC every 2 hours. Pumps shall be refuelled before the tank falls below one quarter.
The discharge shall not be directed into a channel whose flow ratio is 1.0 or above unless the Incident
Commander approves.

## 7 Support to fixed pumps

When a fixed pump at the TU-1 sump or at the D-11 station fails or the fixed pumps cannot keep the water level
falling, the EOC shall treat the site as a priority site under section 3. C-6 shall be called for any power
fault at the station. The mobile pump shall discharge into the same outfall as the fixed pumps when it is free.

## 8 Recovery

When the site is clear and the Incident Commander approves, the crew shall stop the pump, flush and drain it,
recover the hoses and return to the yard. Each pump shall be inspected, refuelled and test-run within 12 hours of
return and marked available only after passing the test. Running hours and faults shall be recorded.
