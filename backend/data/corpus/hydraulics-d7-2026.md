This study was commissioned by the NMC Drainage Division after the Riverside flash flood of 19 July 2025
(HI-2025-FF-01), as recommended by the Post-Incident Review Board in rep-2025-ff-01. It assesses the capacity
of the Kalinadi drain (D-7) as it stands in 2026. It is not the hydraulic review that pol-construction-hazard-2025
section 5 requires before culvert works begin; no such review was made for the 2025 BR-4 works (CH-2025-02).

## 1 Catchment

D-7 drains catchment CT-2 (Hillview–Riverside, 10.6 km2) and the western part of CT-1 (Tekri Upper) that runs
into the head of the channel. The area draining to the BR-4 culvert under Hill Road is 7.8 km2: 5.2 km2 of
Hillview and 2.6 km2 of Tekri Heights. Impervious cover in CT-2 rose from 31 % in 2018 to 44 % in 2026, and in
CT-1 from 8 % to 14 %, with the Hill Road widening, the Hillview Terrace blocks, the growth of the Kalinadi Drain
settlement (RA-02) and the Tekri Quarry. The time of concentration from the head of the catchment to BR-4 is
about 40 minutes.

## 2 Method

Peak flows were computed with the rational method, using rainfall intensities averaged over the 40-minute time
of concentration and runoff coefficients for 2026 land cover that rise as the ground becomes saturated. The
capacity of each reach was computed from cross-sections surveyed in October 2025 with Manning's equation, and
the capacity of the BR-4 culvert as the flow at which it runs full under inlet control. All capacities assume a
free outfall to the Kalinadi and a clear channel unless a blocked fraction is stated.

## 3 Design peak flows at BR-4

| Return period | Intensity over 40 min (mm/h) | Peak flow at BR-4 (m3/s) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 years | 38 | 21 |
| 5 years | 49 | 28 |
| 10 years | 57 | 33 |
| 25 years | 67 | 39 |
| 50 years | 75 | 44 |
| 100 years | 82 | 49 |

The flows are for ground already moist from earlier rain, as is usual in the monsoon; on dry ground they are
about 15 % lower. Flows below BR-4 increase with the Riverside runoff collected by D-8.

## 4 Capacity by reach

| Reach | Capacity (m3/s) |
|---|---|
| Head reach | 45 |
| Hillview reach | 43 |
| BR-4 culvert, 2.4 x 1.8 m since May 2025 | 27 |
| BR-4 culvert as built in 1994, 3.2 x 2.4 m | 44 |
| Riverside reach above D-8 | 42 |
| Riverside reach below D-8 | 50 |

The recorded design capacity of 42 m3/s is that of the Riverside reach above D-8, which controlled the channel
until 2025. Since May 2025 the BR-4 culvert controls it at 27 m3/s, 64 % of the design capacity. At BR-4, 27
m3/s is exceeded by about the 4-year peak flow; the reaches on either side carry about the 40-year flow.

## 5 Effect of blockage

Debris, silt or waste at the culvert inlet, its trash rack or in the channel reduces the open flow area. The
conveyance of a section falls roughly in proportion to its blocked fraction. For the BR-4 culvert this gives
about 20 m3/s at a blocked fraction of 0.25 and 13.5 m3/s at 0.50; for a 42 m3/s reach, about 31 m3/s and
21 m3/s. For comparison, debris from the Hill Road cut landslide of August 2019 (HI-2019-LS-01) blocked 45 % of
the channel, whose capacity was then about 38 m3/s, and the Tekri Quarry bench slide of September 2024
(HI-2024-LS-01) blocked 15 % of the head reach for two weeks.

## 6 The D-8 confluence and the outfall

The Riverside outfall drain D-8, whose capacity fell from 15 to 13 m3/s with the encroachment recorded in
CH-2025-05, discharges into D-7 through a flap gate that closes when the Kalinadi reaches 4.0 m on RV-01. While
the gate is closed, D-8 cannot discharge and Riverside runoff ponds behind it. D-8 also cannot discharge freely
when D-7 below the confluence is obstructed or running full, as in August 2019. The D-7 outfall itself has no
gate. This study did not model backwater from a high Kalinadi; its capacities assume a free outfall, as on
19 July 2025, when the river stood at 3.4 m.

## 7 Back-analysis of the 2025 flash flood

On 19 July 2025 RG-02 recorded 96 mm in 24 hours with a peak intensity of 55 mm/h on ground moist from a week
of rain. The method of section 2 gives a peak flow of 31 m3/s at BR-4, matching the estimate in rep-2025-ff-01
and close to the 8-year flow. Against the culvert capacity of 27 m3/s, a flow ratio of about 1.15, the model
reproduces the surcharge at BR-4 and the backing up of water into the Kalinadi Drain settlement (RA-02). With
the culvert as built in 1994, capacity 44 m3/s, the same flow would have passed BR-4 at a flow ratio of about
0.70 without surcharge.

## 8 Options for the capital programme

The study records three options for the Drainage Division's capital programme: replacing the BR-4 box with a
culvert of at least 7.7 m2 waterway, which restores about 44 m3/s; adding a second 2.4 x 1.8 m cell beside the
existing one, giving about 52 m3/s; and, with either, a debris rack upstream of BR-4 with a clear area at least
four times the culvert opening, so that the rack itself does not become the control. None of the options had
been approved or funded at the date of this study.
